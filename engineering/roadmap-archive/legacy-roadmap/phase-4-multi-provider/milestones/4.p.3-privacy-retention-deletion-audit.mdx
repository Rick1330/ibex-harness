# Milestone 4.P.3 — Privacy, Retention, Deletion & Audit Governance

**Status:** In progress — deletion foundations are present; lifecycle and safe-rendering residuals remain.
**Outcome:** Evidence is useful without crossing the no-prompt/no-completion boundary, and reads/actions expose retention, hold, redaction, deletion, and audit state honestly.
**Track:** [Track P — Operator Readiness, Evidence & Control Plane](/roadmap/phase-4-multi-provider/tracks#track-p--operator-readiness-evidence--control-plane)
**Dependencies:** 4.P.1; 4.P.2
**Estimated effort:** 3 weeks
**Gate:** G4 governance

<Callout type="note" title="Track D lifecycle contract">
  D2–D6 must treat lifecycle as data, not presentation: every read identifies retention status, legal-hold status, redaction/capture mode, deletion/tombstone state, freshness, and whether the source is complete. A missing or expired item is not rendered as a successful empty result. Mutations return a job/receipt state and never imply completion before every deployed store is verified.
</Callout>

## Contracts and current foundations

The privacy governance migration defines capture policies, metadata-only defaults, legal holds, an org-deletion saga, per-store receipts, audit append/verifier behavior, encrypted object-store writes, and asynchronous redaction. Deployed stores are configured explicitly; omitted stores may be `not_applicable`, while unreachable configured stores fail closed. Soft-deleted organizations still require explicit evidence and session-event deletion.

Audit records include actor, `org_id`, purpose, assurance, policy result, action, and receipt references. Privileged raw/export/deletion access remains server-authorized and step-up protected.

## Residuals

ClickHouse TTL is storage hygiene rather than a deletion SLA and may age rows during an active hold; broker/result and backup erasure, crypto twin parity, and a retention scheduler/API remain open follow-ups. Full safe rendering, hostile-content handling, and DOM sanitization are Track D/P6 dependencies. Usage and billing ledgers are a documented non-erasable carve-out until their lifecycle contract changes.

## Verification gate

Prove cross-store deletion receipts, hold blocking, tombstone behavior, redaction labels, audit verification, and post-deletion reads across two tenants. P6 adds hostile-content and redaction browser checks; D2–D6 must display residual states rather than hide them.

## Rollout and rollback

**Trigger:** incomplete receipt, hold bypass, unsafe rendering, or deletion/read mismatch. **Reversal:** block destructive actions, retain legal holds and audit history, and return to metadata-only reads. **Verify:** rerun store receipts, hold tests, hostile-content checks, and tenant-negative reads.
