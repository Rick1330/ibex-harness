Deletion is a lifecycle, not a single `DELETE`. The platform distinguishes immediate read suppression from physical purge, and it distinguishes an organization suspension from a deletion job. Confusing those states is how deleted data reappears through a cache or projection.

## Memory lifecycle

The target memory flow is:

```text
authenticated source
  → validate/scope/size
  → PII and safety checks
  → candidate observation
  → exact idempotency/hash dedup
  → near-duplicate/temporal/conflict analysis
  → active | quarantine | supersede | expire | delete
```

Every observation is intended to carry tenant/resource scope, actor/source, content hash, observed/processed/valid times, source reference, status, retention or legal hold, model/embedding/preprocessing identities, and links to duplicates, conflicts, or supersession.

The durable substrate exists locally, but the full append-only provenance ledger and accepted public lifecycle contract do not. GAP-007 remains open.

### Forget

`POST /v1/memories/{memory_id}/forget` is a **provisional** revision-fenced tombstone:

- The caller must supply the expected lifecycle revision.
- The row is marked tombstoned with a new revision.
- Reads, vector search, full-text search, hot cache, feedback, dedup, and conflict candidate loads filter on `deleted_at IS NULL` and `tombstoned = FALSE`.
- Cache eviction happens after the authoritative tombstone commits.
- A stale revision is rejected rather than overwriting a newer lifecycle state.

This is not yet an accepted public deletion contract. Full-store fan-out, projection fencing, restore behavior, and independent verification remain open.

## Organization lifecycle

| Operation | Route | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspend | `POST /v1/organizations/{org_id}/suspend` | Marks the organization suspended and publishes invalidation; owner-level permission required |
| Request deletion | `DELETE /v1/organizations/{org_id}` | Returns `202` with a deletion job; enqueue failure is fail-closed |
| Poll job | `GET /v1/organizations/{org_id}/deletion-jobs/{job_id}` | Returns job status and store receipts |

Deletion is executed by a worker saga with a short soft time limit and a longer hard limit. The job claims work, checks legal holds under an advisory lock, resolves archived object URIs before the PostgreSQL purge, purges canonical rows, publishes model-policy invalidation, runs optional store stages, and finalizes only when receipts satisfy the deployment profile.

### Store receipts

The saga records per-store status rather than claiming one global success:

| Receipt state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `verified` | The deployed store stage completed and its digest/count checks passed |
| `failed` | The stage failed or a deployed store was unreachable |
| `not_applicable` | The store is not deployed for this profile |

A deployed store is not finalized on `not_applicable`. An undeployed store may be `not_applicable`; it is never counted as verified. ClickHouse, Redis, and object-store stages are optional only when their runtime configuration says they are not deployed.

The PostgreSQL stage removes evidence/outbox rows, policy and credential rows, rate-limit overrides, TOTP secrets, operator ledger rows, memory rows, sessions, directives, tokens, agents, invites, and users, then marks the organization `cancelled`. Memory deletion emits a `purged` lifecycle outbox event. Active legal holds are never deleted mid-saga.

## Legal holds

Legal holds are set and cleared through the management API:

- Listing requires a tenant-scoped operator token.
- Setting or clearing requires `LegalHoldManage` and a session subject.
- An active hold blocks physical purge but **does not restore retrieval eligibility** for an already tombstoned row.
- Deletion checks the hold under the same advisory lock used by hold creation, so a hold cannot land between the check and the purge stage.
- A blocked job ends as `hold_blocked`, not as a successful deletion.

## Retention

The accepted evidence-contract baseline is 30 days after `occurred_at`, with legal or incident holds overriding deletion. This is a contract default; runtime retention enforcement and deletion receipts are not proven across every store.

Retention must not be inferred from a ClickHouse TTL, object-store lifecycle rule, or projection completeness. Those are implementation details, not authority.

## What is proven locally

- Revision-fenced memory tombstones and read filters.
- Post-response cache invalidation after the tombstone commit.
- A receipted PostgreSQL-first organization deletion saga.
- Legal-hold blocking and job status reporting.
- Optional ClickHouse, Redis, and object-store stages when configured.

## What is not proven

- A complete per-store deletion certificate with restore verification.
- Atomic fencing between deletion and every in-flight replay or projection write.
- Full object-store and backup-copy purge.
- Hosted or HA deletion recovery.
- Legal-hold release followed by a verified purge drill.
- A public, accepted memory lifecycle including checkpoints and provenance.

## Operator checklist

- [ ] Determine whether the request is suspension, forget, or organization deletion.
- [ ] Check for active legal holds before promising a purge.
- [ ] Record the deletion job ID and poll receipts; do not infer completion from the `202`.
- [ ] Verify each deployed store reports `verified`, not merely `not_applicable`.
- [ ] Confirm tombstoned rows stay unreadable while caches and projections rebuild.
- [ ] Preserve IDs and outcomes; do not log memory content or secrets.
